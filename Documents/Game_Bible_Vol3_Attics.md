# THE WEEPING WALLS — GAME BIBLE, VOLUME 3
## Ashford Manor: The Attics · The House's Memory Loft

The attic opens when the player chooses to use the **chatelaine** on the attic stair door (Servants' Passage, west end) — available from the Act 1 finale, intended to be explored across Acts 2–3. It is the most nonlinear floor: no act-gate inside it except the **Phantom Stair**, which refuses everyone until Act 4.

**Floor theme:** *Storage.* Everything the house has absorbed gets kept somewhere. The attic is where the player finally understands that the manor is not haunted — it is **archival** — two floors before the cellar makes it explicit.

**Canon from the surveyed plan:** the hatched zones are under-eaves crawl space (traversable crouching, lamp-only, the floor's scare arteries); the central **void — no access** is drawn with unbroken walls; surveyor **Caton's** hurried second hand appears again ("measured thrice. it is larger inside the measurements."); and at the east end a stair is marked **To Ground Floor** that appears on no other floor's plan. All of it is true.

---

## 3.1 THE ATTIC STAIR + DOOR
**Read:** Steep, narrow, treads worn to a shine in the centre. The door at the top is low — even Mara ducks. The chatelaine's largest key fits.
**Interactive:** The stairs creak a five-note pattern ascending — the **lullaby's first five notes** (the same pattern as the Back Stair; the house hums its one song in wood). A player who has solved the Nursery music box gets a journal note from Mara: *"The stairs know it too."*
**Recorder yield (left on the stair):** ascending footsteps that never arrive; descending footsteps that never left.

## 3.2 LONG ATTIC — THE SPINE
**Read:** The roof's whole length in one room: rafters diving to the eaves, dust-sheeted shapes ranked like a congregation, daylight in blades through slate gaps. The bell wires from the floors below run visibly along the rafters, stapled in neat Victorian runs.
**Dressing:** Sheeted furniture (each sheet liftable — mirrors, a rocking chair, a pram; one sheet, lifted, has **nothing under it and keeps its shape for a held second** before collapsing — scripted once, never repeated, never explained); a child's chalk hopscotch on the boards, numbered 1–13.
**Interactive:** THE WIRE TRACE (keystone puzzle, below); the dust sheets; the crawl spaces (entries at four points, connecting the attic's rooms off-corridor; inside: insulation, mummified wasp nests, and at one turn a **view down through a ceiling crack into the unnumbered guest bedroom** — the only way to observe that room "unattended": the bed is turned down, and the indentation of someone sitting on its edge slowly relaxes as you watch).
**Puzzle:** **THE WIRE TRACE** — the blank bell's wire (Servants' Passage bell board, Vol 2) enters the attic and runs the rafters. The player physically follows it — over beams, into and out of crawl space, a lamp-lit traversal puzzle — past every junction where the *labelled* bells' wires branch sensibly down into walls. The blank wire branches nowhere. It runs the attic's full length and **enters the void room's wall**, dead centre, through a hole too neat to be damage. Completing the trace pins a new exhibit to the evidence board: *"The thirteenth bell rings from the room with no door."* (Deliberately not a lock-and-key puzzle; this is evidence-as-gameplay — the player's legs do the deduction.)
**Recorder yield:** Wind through slates — and beneath it, faint, the sound of **paper being shelved**: sheet after sheet, patiently, somewhere behind the void wall.

## 3.3 WATER TANK ROOM
**Read:** The house's iron heart: a riveted cistern on timber cradles, pipes diving into the floor, the air mineral and cold. Trunks crowd the corners — overflow storage, exactly as drawn.
**Interactive:** THE TANK AS LISTENING ORGAN — every wet point in the house terminates here: the Washroom's self-steaming mirror, the Conservatory's never-stale watering can, the endlessly draining tub. Pressing the recorder to the tank's flank yields a composite of every water sound in the manor at once — threaded through them, clearest underwater, **the lullaby hummed in full** (the alternate source for the Nursery music-box solution: every audio puzzle in the game has two sources — the design rule that keeps the recorder fair); THE DRAIN SEQUENCE (puzzle); the trunks (one holds the **toy theatre's missing cut-out figure** — Volume 2's optional payoff).
**Puzzle:** **THE DRAIN SEQUENCE** — three valves (inflow, house feed, waste) worked in the right order lower the tank without flooding the feed pipes; the order is stamped on a maintenance plate gone green with verdigris — legible by taking a **rubbing** (paper + pencil from the kitchen, a physical interaction). Tank lowered, its floor holds a **soldered tin**, decades submerged, contents bone-dry: the **Roof Walk hatch key** and a child's **marble bag** (1924; one marble is not glass, and the game does not describe it further).
**Items:** **Roof Walk key**; **marble bag** (the not-glass marble matters in 3.5; returning the bag to Guest Bedroom II's toy arrangement also completes the game's quietest optional scene).
**Recorder yield:** The full lullaby, underwater — the cleanest capture in the house, cleaner than the Conservatory's. Water remembers best.

## 3.4 SERVANT'S SICK ROOM (NORTH) — THE REAL ONE
**Read:** A whitewashed sliver of mercy: iron bed, washstand, a fever chart on a nail, a small shuttered window. Honest, sad, ordinary.
**Interactive:** THE FEVER CHART: *Ada P—, taken to bed 9th March 1893* — temperatures in a doctor's hand, annotations: *"claims the bells ring themselves"* · *"claims the corridor is longer at night"* — and a discharge line: *"resumed duties 22nd March."* Ada recovered. Ada's diary (Vol 2) corroborates: she lived, left service, left the house. The north room is proof the house lets people go.
**Items:** **Fever chart (north)** — evidence.
**Recorder yield:** A young woman's breathing, congested, easing night by night across the recording's minutes — time compressed; recovery, archived.

## 3.5 SERVANT'S SICK ROOM (SOUTH) — THE COPY
**Read:** The plan labels two rooms identically, and the plan is right: this room is the north room **again** — iron bed, washstand, chart on its nail, shutter — every object mirrored left-for-right, as if printed from the other's negative.
**Interactive:** THE SECOND FEVER CHART: *Ada P—, taken to bed 9th March 1893* — same patient, same hand… but the annotations continue past the 22nd, and the final line is not a discharge: *"deceased 2:47, the 29th."* Two charts. One survivor, one death — the same woman. **Both pin to the evidence board, and the board draws its first red string: CONTRADICTION.** The game's thesis as paperwork: the house keeps the version that wasn't witnessed. Out there, Ada lived. In here, the unwitnessed Ada is still filed; THE MIRROR-LINK: the rooms are entangled — move the north washstand's cup, the south cup moves, mirrored; open the north shutter, the south shutter opens. One-directional by day (north drives south); the direction **reverses after 2:47**.
**Puzzle:** **THE MIRROR CHEST** — under the south bed, a trunk rusted shut, lid fused, unopenable forever. Under the north bed, its twin: sound, and locked. Its lock takes the **not-glass marble** — a key disguised as a marble, the 1924 child's hiding trick. Unlock and open the **north** chest, and the **south** chest swings open with it. Only the south one has contents: **CATON'S FIELD BOOK.**
**Items:** **CATON'S FIELD BOOK** — the attic's grail. The 1885 surveyor's working notebook: every room measured twice — the figures he submitted, and the figures he *got* — marginalia degrading from professional to frightened: *"Caton Pillar bears more than the load above it"* · *"the void admits my chain but not my person"* · *"the walls respire. five seconds the cycle. I timed it."* · *"I have signed the plans. God forgive the plans."* **Mechanical payoff:** the Field Book upgrades the tape measure — the map UI now overlays Caton's true figures against the player's own measurements. The Compass Lie stops being atmosphere and becomes a readable system: every lying room in the manor is now findable.
**Recorder yield (south room):** the north room's recording, played back — already on the tape before the player records it.
**Role:** The duplicate-room gift from your map, weaponised: the clearest statement of what the house does, plus the endgame's measurement system.

## 3.6 THE VOID — NO ACCESS
**Read:** A rectangle of wall in the attic's heart with no door, no hatch, no seam. The blank bell's wire enters it. Caton's chain entered it. Nothing else ever has.
**Interactive:** Measured with the Field Book overlay, the dark joke lands: exterior walls say 9 by 12 feet; Caton's chain, fed through the wire hole, paid out **41 feet before it stopped** — and the overlay renders the void as a room-shaped hole the map refuses to fill; the recorder against its wall: the 2:47 voice, **louder here than anywhere in the manor — and it is the wrong voice.** Every 2:47 capture in the game is Eleanor. This one is **male**, measured, patient — *dictating*. (Players will assume Thomas. Volume 4 neither confirms nor denies; the dictating voice is the series-finale thread — the thing all the network's nodes share.); the wire hole (peer in: no light returns; post the recorder through on its strap and pull it back: the recording holds **shelving sounds, and the player's own name, spelled out letter by letter, as if being filed**).
**Puzzle:** None. **The void never opens.** Hard design law: no key, no act, no ending opens this room from the attic. (Its interior is touched once, obliquely, in one of Volume 4's epilogues — only there.) Rooms that never open are the rooms players still post about years later.
**Recorder yield:** Dictation — patient, male, endless. One legible phrase per act, escalating: Act 2 — *"…item: one woman, grey eyes…"* · Act 3 — *"…item: one journalist, lapsed…"* · Act 4 — *"…item: pending."*

## 3.7 HATCH AND LADDER — THE ROOF WALK
**Read:** A square hatch, an iron ladder, a padlock for the **Roof Walk key**. Above: a leaded walkway between roof pitches, chimneys like standing stones, the county to the horizon — and the front parapet.
**Interactive:** **THE THIRTEENTH WINDOW (the count resolved):** from the parapet, with the interior tally from Maid's Room IV (Vol 2) in hand, Mara counts the façade from above and outside: **thirteen windows, twelve rooms.** The camera marks each as she counts — and the thirteenth maps to **no interior wall the player has ever stood at**. Its glass is dust-blind by day. Return at night (the clock mechanic): at **2:47** the thirteenth window **lights** — warm lamplight, a figure's shadow crossing it, twice, pacing — in a window belonging to nowhere. Photographing it creates the single highest-value exhibit on the evidence board, and the only photograph in the game that, reviewed later at the kitchen table, **shows the window dark**. The board accepts it anyway, under a string-label the player has never seen before: ***WITNESSED — UNPROVABLE.*** The novel's whole theme, arriving in the UI.
**Items:** the photograph; a **slate fragment** etched with a mason's tally — thirteen strokes, the thirteenth struck sideways (pocket lore).
**Recorder yield (on the walk, 2:47):** the pacing, through slate — and below it, very far down, the **cellar door's bolt trying itself**. The roof can hear the cellar. The house is one instrument.
**Role:** The vertical climax of the exterior mystery; the series' Episode 1 thirteen-windows image converted into the game's best night set piece.

## 3.8 THE PHANTOM STAIR — "TO GROUND FLOOR" (EAST)
**Read:** At the attic's east end, exactly as the plan dares to mark it: a stair descending. It appears on no other floor's plan. The First Floor's torn plan-edge is torn *precisely* where this stair would pass through it.
**Interactive (Acts 2–3):** The player can descend. Down, turn, down — ending at a **blank plastered wall** after exactly **28 steps** (the Field Book flags it: 28 feet of stair serving zero floors — the same figure as the sealed corridor's exterior; the house reuses its numbers like a composer reuses motifs). Climb back up: **the count is 30.** Every descent-and-return gains steps. This is the one place the geometry is allowed to move *while the player is inside it*, and it is used with total restraint: the step-count creep is the only event. No scares are staged here, because the arithmetic is the scare.
**Gate:** **Act 4 only · 2:47 only · lamp extinguished** — three conditions, each separately clued (Whitmore's "it likes industry," inverted by Caton's margin: *"it admits idleness. arrive with nothing lit"*; the void's *"item: pending"*; and the cellar door's opening, which re-tunes the whole house). Descending dark at 2:47 in Act 4, the stair does not end. It arrives **behind the Bricked Archway in the cellar** — the far side of the one wall the cellar never opens. The Phantom Stair is the house's private circulation: attic to foundations, memory to archive, bypassing every floor where people lived. Usable exactly once; afterwards, 28 steps and a wall, forever.
**Recorder yield:** descending footsteps matching the player's own, offset by one step — and beneath them, ascending footsteps that do not.
**Role:** The attic's act-gate and the bridge into Volume 4. The only fast-travel in the game, and it travels somewhere the player will wish it hadn't.

# 4 · FLOOR PUZZLE ECONOMY

Chatelaine (Vol 2) → ATTIC DOOR → Long Attic: **WIRE TRACE** → void wall (thesis exhibit) · Water Tank: rubbing → **DRAIN SEQUENCE** → tin → **Roof Walk key + marble-key** · Sick Rooms: marble-key → north chest → **south chest → CATON'S FIELD BOOK** → measurement overlay (endgame system) + the Ada CONTRADICTION exhibit · Roof Walk: **THIRTEENTH WINDOW** night photograph → *witnessed — unprovable* · Phantom Stair: step-creep (Acts 2–3) → **Act 4 dark descent → behind the Bricked Archway** (Volume 4's entry beat).

Both audio-critical solutions (the lullaby) have their redundant second source on this floor (the tank), honouring the two-sources rule.

# 5 · BUILD NOTES
Light the floor in blades and pools: slate-gap daylight by day, lamp-radius by night; crawl spaces are lamp-cone corridors. The void's exterior wall should be the **cleanest surface in the attic** — no dust, no cobwebs; the house maintains it, and observant players will read that. The Phantom Stair's step-count must be literal, countable, and honest in both directions: players WILL count frames. Let them. The arithmetic being verifiably true is the floor's contract with the player.
