# THE WEEPING WALLS — CONTINUITY & LORE AUDIT
## Novel (Book 1, full text) vs. Game Bible Volumes 1–4 (+ series scripts)

Verdict up front: the game is structurally faithful. Every load-bearing element of the novel is present in the bibles. The work needed falls into three buckets: **(A)** novel material the game should absorb, **(B)** game inventions to mark as deliberate additions, and **(C)** contradictions inside the manuscript itself that need an authorial ruling before they fossilise into game canon.

# 2 · IN THE NOVEL, MISSING FROM THE GAME — RECOMMENDED ADDITIONS

## 2.1 MARTIN CALDWELL — the keeper's agent ★ highest priority
Ch.7: sixtyish, wool coat, briefcase, "Caldwell and Associates — we manage the estate." Knocks at exactly **2:47 PM** on December 1st. Recognises the black book mid-sentence and recovers. And the devastating detail: **his own entry on page 41 — "Martin Caldwell — September 1999 — Location: Village — Status: Living."** Not keeper, not victim: the recruiter. "There's always someone who needs it, Ms. Voss. There always has been."
**Game patch:** make Caldwell the human face of the recruitment lore — (a) his signature on every Box Room rental agreement and every tenancy in the label backups; (b) a scripted **December 1st visit event** (the game's only other living NPC, arriving at 2:47 PM — players who've internalised the time will feel it before they can say why); (c) page 41 of the black book as a findable evidence beat; (d) his "Status: Living" line as a publish-meter exhibit. He also gives the village solicitor's office (already in your environment sheets) its owner.

## 2.2 MARA'S OWN DEATH DATE — the December 2nd deadline ★ highest priority
Ch.6: the book writes **"Mara Voss, 02/12, AH"** — three days out. Ch.9: she survives the date by publishing, and the entry rewrites itself to **"December 2nd: Incomplete."** Not a death date — *a category the house has no precedent for.*
**Game patch:** this is a gift of a mechanic and currently absent. Recommended: when the player first reads the black book's final page in Act 3, the date appears — and the in-game calendar (the clock/pendulum system already exists in Vol 2) starts visibly counting toward it. Act 4 must be entered before the date; the three endings resolve what the entry finally says: serve → a keeper's entry in her own hand; destroy → "attempted destruction. Filed."; publish → **"Incomplete"**, the word the whole novel pivots on. (Note: the episode scripts currently say her name "didn't have a death date" — Episode 5's outro needs a one-line fix to match the book.)

## 2.3 THE DAUGHTER — "Not to me. To Eleanor. To our daughter."
Ch.4: the journal's first page is addressed to the daughter — also named Eleanor — sent to Thomas's sister **in Boston**, "raised never knowing what her father is."
**Game patch:** currently nowhere in the game. Minimum: the journal text quoted faithfully in Vol 5; the Nursery (ground floor) and the wing's second nursery gain their true meaning — one prepared, one never used. Maximum: the Boston line is sequel fuel (a descendant in the network era) and one optional Morning Room letter. Either way it cannot stay missing — it's the emotional engine of Eleanor's first page.

## 2.4 THE BREATHING WALLS
Ch.1: the house breathes — five seconds in, five seconds out, fractional but palpable under the palm.
**Game patch:** trivially cheap, hugely on-brand: a global ambient system (subtle wall displacement + sound bed on a 10-second cycle), and one tutorial interaction in the kitchen — *press your palm to the wall* — that becomes the player's recurring "is it still breathing?" check. The breath should slow after publication (Ch.9: "the breathing in the walls had slowed").

## 2.5 ELEANOR'S PORTRAIT — the scratched nameplate
Ch.3: entrance hall portrait, brass plate **"Eleanor Ashford, 1862–1887," two lines scratched through the plate** with something sharp; Ch.14: the portrait is rescued and rehung above the archive. 
**Game patch:** add to the Entrance Hall (Vol 1) as an early examine: the scratched plate is the first act of attempted erasure the player touches. Epilogue states: in serve/publish endings the portrait hangs over the kitchen evidence board; the scratches remain — testimony of the attempt.

## 2.6 THE ANONYMOUS TESTIMONY SHELF
Ch.11: a library shelf of blank-spined books, every one filled with dense handwriting, no attribution. "Some things were for her alone."
**Game patch:** add to the Library (Vol 1): a shelf the player can pull volumes from endlessly — procedurally fragmentary testimony, never the same page twice, **cannot be photographed** (camera returns blur) — the one collection the evidence board refuses. Pure dread economy, one room over from the warm book.

## 2.7 THE CONSERVATORY GROWS LEMONS, NOT ROSES
Ch.4: Eleanor's blood soaks "into the soil **around the lemon trees**." The rose scent belongs to the wing's locked fourth door.
**Game patch:** Vol 1's Conservatory should be re-dressed: dead **lemon trees** in rows, one impossibly alive (fruit ripening since 1887) replacing the living rose; the rose motif stays exclusively with the sealed wing — which actually sharpens both: citrus = the murder room, roses = where she waits.

## 2.8 JAMES KELLER & THE THREE CROWNS
Ch.6: Keller is landlord of the **Three Crowns in Millworth** (the next town), not the Ashford Arms; 43, divorced, daughter at Manchester; everyone in Millworth "keeps count" of the house's tenants; he supplies the prior-keeper chain (Whitmore 3 months, Sarah Chen, David Porter, Margaret Simms, Richard Vance).
**Game patch:** the game is single-location, so Keller can't be a visitable NPC — but his book entry (12/01, six weeks out, in Vol 1's lore) should be joined by his *counting* lore: a Three Crowns beer mat or note in Whitmore's effects listing the recent tenants in a pub landlord's hand. (Series patch: Episode 6 already matches Keller correctly.)

## 2.9 THE TWO BOOKS ARE TWO DIFFERENT KINDS OF BOOK
Novel: the **black book** = the register (names, dates, locations); the **cellar book** = the keepers' own log — entries by keepers from 1823 on, including Thomas's 1885 entry ("The house does not kill. The house preserves… E. knows. That is sufficient.") and Eleanor's three-sentence 1887 entry ("I told the truth. No one believed me. The house believed me."), pages that multiply, compressed microscopic script, blank onion-skin pages waiting.
**Game patch:** Vol 4 currently frames the cellar book as the "master register." Reframe: the Foundation Chamber book is the **keepers' testament** — the register's other half — and the Act 4 reading beat should surface Thomas's and Eleanor's entries verbatim. The serve ending's typed line then lands as the next entry in a 200-year sequence the player has just read.

## 2.10 THE NOVEL'S TRUE ENDING IS A SERVE/PUBLISH HYBRID — keep the decomposition, but honour it
Ch.12–14: Mara publishes AND stays; writes archive entries at 2:47 AM **that she does not remember writing**; "Can I leave?" / "I don't know. No one has tried."; the cellar book gains lines in her hand, then in the original keeper's hand ("You are not the first to see it"), then in a hand no one knows, ink wet: **"We know you are there. We have always known."**
**Game patch:** the game's clean three-way split is the right adaptation — but the publish ending's epilogue should inherit the novel's shadow: one post-credit beat where the kitchen ledger contains an entry in Mara's hand, timestamped 2:47 AM, that the player never wrote. Triumph, with the novel's asterisk.

## 2.11 SMALLER PICKUPS
- The **eighth key that appears on the ring** (Ch.3) vs. the game's Chapel-puzzle iron key: keep the puzzle (players need agency), but add the examine line when the niche opens: *"The key ring in her pocket is heavier. She didn't put it there."* — the house granting, as in the book.
- **"I will tell you what happened"** looped at the wing threshold + the pen scratching (Ch.3): make this the sealed-wing door's pre-Act-3 recorder yield in Vol 1 (currently the door yields only silence; the novel gives it this exact loop once the key exists).
- The **read receipt at 4:52 AM** (Ch.9): already in the episode scripts; add to the publish ending's send sequence as the held-breath beat before the LED blink.
- **Two articles, not one** (Ch.11–12): the investigation piece and the personal account ("The Archive of Eleanor Ashford: A Document of Residence"); the split public reaction (hoax vs revelation). Worth one line in the publish epilogue's email montage.
- The **Prague key arriving warm** and "the accumulation is portable" (Ch.14): perfect post-game item — it can arrive in the manor's letterbox in any ending's epilogue, seeding Book/Season 2's object-node lore.

# 4 · CONTRADICTIONS INSIDE THE MANUSCRIPT — AUTHORIAL RULINGS NEEDED ⚠
The draft disagrees with itself on points the game must lock down. Each item: the conflict, and a recommended ruling.

1. **Who Eleanor is.** Ch.1's history: Thomas's *wife is Catherine*, Eleanor is the *daughter*, died at 17. Everywhere else: Eleanor is the wife. → **Rule: Eleanor is the wife** (delete/revise Ch.1's Catherine paragraph; or keep "Catherine" as Thomas's *mother* for flavour).
2. **Eleanor's age and dates.** Newspaper: 29. Coroner: 24. Interview: born April 1862, died *November* 1887, "twenty-five." Portrait plate: 1862–1887. Death date elsewhere: **September 14, 1887** (used everywhere structurally). → **Rule: born 1862, died September 14, 1887, aged 25.** (Or deliberately keep ONE discrepancy — the coroner's "24" — as Whitmore's sloppy lie, a findable error in the false report. That's a free clue.)
3. **How she died, and why.** Journal (Ch.4): murdered **in the conservatory**, skull, because of the book. Interview (Ch.11): pushed **down the stairs**, neck, because she exposed **mill embezzlement**. Official record: staircase fall. → **Rule (recommended): the conservatory is the truth; "the staircase" is Thomas's lie, repeated so long even the official story believes it.** The embezzlement either goes, or becomes the *cover motive* Thomas seeded (he'd rather be thought a murderer-for-money than a keeper). The Ch.11 interview needs revising to match — as written, Eleanor contradicts her own journal.
4. **When Mara arrives.** Ch.1: April 16. Ch.2 playback: "October third." Ch.6–7: late November, dying Dec 2. Ledger: "Arrived November 28, **2019**." Black book: "Mara Voss, October **2023**." → **Rule: one arrival in late October 2023; the deadline December 2, 2023.** (The game and series already run on October arrival → December climax.)
5. **Caldwell's identity split.** Ch.7: estate agent and recruiter. Ch.9: suddenly an *author* whose "publisher is recalling the book," with editor Catherine Chen / Morrison & Vale — a literary-fraud subplot from what looks like an earlier draft. → **Rule: Caldwell is the estate agent only.** Catherine Chen survives as the publishing contact, but she's commissioning *Mara's* account, not exposing Caldwell's plagiarism. Ch.9 needs the heaviest revision in the book.
6. **Dara's location.** Ch.13 letter: a warehouse **in Edinburgh**. Ch.14 email: "seventeen storage units in **western Sydney**." → Pick one (Sydney is what Episode 10 and the network montage use).
7. **The pubs.** Ashford Arms (village, old landlord) and Three Crowns (Millworth, Keller) are both used; Ch.6's first line confuses them. → Keep both, as two characters/two towns — just make each scene name the right pub.
8. **Keeper tenure.** Keller's list: days-to-months. Ch.7's spreadsheet: average 14 months, shortest 8. → **Rule: Keller counts *all tenants* (most flee fast); the spreadsheet counts *keepers who took the duty* (they last 8–26 months).** One clarifying sentence fixes it, and it's actually richer.
9. **136 vs 137 years; "no phone line" vs sending email from the study.** Standardise to 136 (1887→2023), and establish the house "allows" connectivity intermittently — which the game can use (the laptop only connects after the choice scene; the house controls the wire, like everything else).
10. **The wing's contents vs the ground-floor map.** The novel puts the nursery/sewing room *inside* the wing; your game map puts a Nursery on the ground floor. → Already reconciled in the bibles: the ground-floor Nursery is the *prepared* nursery; the wing holds the *never-used second* nursery. Worth one line of lore (twins expected? the house duplicating again?) — recommend the latter: it rhymes with the Sick Rooms.

The manuscript itself needs ten rulings (§4), most of them one-sentence fixes, before anything else is built on top of it. Decide §4, and I'll apply §5 in one pass.
