# THE WEEPING WALLS — GAME BIBLE, VOLUME 1
## Ashford Manor: Ground Floor · Room Breakdown · Puzzle Economy · Map Prompts

First-person investigation horror. The player is Mara Voss. The verbs are: **examine, record, measure, collect, combine, unlock.** No combat — the house is not trying to kill you. It is trying to be *witnessed*, and it gates your progress until you've understood enough to deserve the next room.

---

# 1 · THE STRUCTURE OF THE GAME (how this floor fits the whole)

**The Compass Lie (core mechanic):** Every document, every NPC line, the key tag, Price's warning — all say "the west wing." The map compass shows the sealed wing is EAST. The corridor labelled *West Wing Hall* runs along the genuinely western side. Nothing in the house agrees with the compass. The player who notices early is rewarded; the player who doesn't gets the reveal in Act 3 when they pace the sealed corridor and the measurements don't exist. **Design rule: all in-game text says "west wing"; only the compass rose and the player's own measuring tool tell the truth.**

**The three blocked routes off this floor:**

| Route | Leads to | Blocked by | Opens in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back Stair (central corridor) | 2nd Floor West — servants' quarters, then attic | Painted-shut door + servant's lock; key trapped in the dumbwaiter | Act 1 finale |
| Grand Staircase (Entrance Hall) | 2nd Floor East — family rooms | Fallen chandelier wedged across the stairs; winch above is missing its handle and rope | Act 2 opening |
| Cellar Stairs (off West Wing Hall) | Cellar — the foundations, the original book | A door with **no lock and no handle** that simply will not open. It is held from the inside. | Act 4 — a story gate, not an item gate (see §4) |

**The fourth lock** — the iron-banded double doors to the sealed wing (map: LOCKED EAST WING) — is the Act 3 climax of the ground floor, opened by the iron key won from the Chapel puzzle chain.

**The Recorder (signature mechanic):** Mara's recorder can be placed in any room and left running while she's elsewhere. Returning and playing it back yields room-specific audio: clues, fragments of Eleanor, lies the house tells. At 2:47 AM (the game runs a clock), every recording captures *something*. Several puzzles are audio-solved. The Conservatory gives the strongest signal in the house.

**The Tape Measure (second mechanic):** found in the Study. Any corridor or room can be paced/measured. Most rooms measure true. The ones that don't are telling you something.

# 2 · ROOM-BY-ROOM BREAKDOWN (GROUND FLOOR)

Format per room — *Read* (what the player sees), *Dressing* (non-interactive atmosphere), *Interactive*, *Items* (inventory), *Puzzle*, *Recorder yield* (what taping this room gives you), *Role*.

## 2.1 ENTRANCE HALL
**Read:** Two storeys of dark panelling and fern-and-songbird wallpaper. The grand staircase sweeps up the east side — and halfway up, the great brass chandelier lies smashed across the steps, its chain snapped, crystal scattered like ice. Mara's two suitcases sit abandoned on the flagstones. Above, the broken chain swings from a winch mount in the ceiling.
**Dressing:** Coat stand with one Victorian gentleman's coat nobody claims; umbrella urn; dead grandfather clock stopped at 2:47; dust bar of light from over the door; **Eleanor's portrait** — a woman in her late twenties, 1880s dress, painted with more care than the flanking landscapes deserve. Brass plate: *Eleanor Ashford, 1862–1887* — **two lines scratched through the plate** with something sharp, the name still legible. The first act of attempted erasure the player touches (examine pins it to the board). Epilogue note: in the serve and publish endings the portrait hangs over the kitchen evidence board; the scratches remain — testimony of the attempt.
**Interactive:** The chandelier (examine: "It didn't fall. The chain links are *opened*, not snapped."); the winch mount (missing its crank handle and hoist rope); the grandfather clock (opens — pendulum missing, a folded note inside: Price's inventory sheet listing "13 windows — recount disputed"); front door (always openable — leaving is allowed, an exit-confirmation that doubles as the quit menu, with Price's voice: "Most do leave, you know. The house doesn't mind. It keeps the deposit.").
**Items:** **Oil lamp** (primary light source); **Price's card** (lore).
**Puzzle:** THE CHANDELIER WINCH — combine **brass crank handle** (Study) + **hoist rope** (Library chimney) on the winch mount, raise the chandelier, clear the Grand Staircase. *Gates 2nd Floor East.*
**Recorder yield:** A woman's footsteps descending the blocked stairs, unhurried, passing *through* where the chandelier hangs.
**Role:** Hub. The clock stuck at 2:47 plants the night mechanic in minute one.

## 2.2 DINING ROOM
**Read:** The long mahogany table laid for twelve, cobwebs bridging the candelabra without touching a single glass. Eleven place cards stand at eleven settings. The twelfth setting — head of table — has none.
**Dressing:** Family portraits along both walls (the faces matter — see puzzle); tarnished sideboard; drawn brocade drapes that cannot be opened.
**Interactive:** Each place card (names readable — all are names the player will later find in the black book, dated 1654–1887); each portrait (nameplates + dates); the sideboard (locked, a five-letter rotating-cylinder lock).
**Items:** **Silver letter opener** (tool — opens sealed envelopes and slit wallpaper later); **candle stubs ×3** (for the Chapel).
**Puzzle:** THE TWELFTH GUEST — the eleven place cards must be matched to the eleven portraits by cross-referencing dates on the nameplates. Done correctly, the arrangement spells a question: who sits at the empty head setting? The portraits' eyes all angle toward the head of table. Placing the card the player must *write themselves* (a blank card + pencil are in the drawer) with the name **ELEANOR** clicks the sideboard lock open: inside, the **mourning brooch** (jet, matching her portrait — needed in the Master Bedroom) and a menu dated *September 14th, 1887 — cancelled*.
**Recorder yield:** Cutlery sounds, glasses, a dinner that never happened — and one chair scraping back, hard.
**Role:** Teaches cross-referencing — the game's core intellectual verb — in a safe room.

## 2.3 LIBRARY
**Read:** Floor-to-ceiling shelves of spines faded to a uniform grey-brown, as though the books have been drained. A stone fireplace big enough to stand in, grate full of pale ash. Two leather armchairs worn to pewter.
**Dressing:** A reading stand with an open atlas (the county page is missing); dust in the light bar; a ladder on a rail that moves on its own when unobserved (subtle — players argue about whether it moved).
**Interactive:** The shelves (hundreds of "cold" books, all blank-ish, generic — except **one book that is warm to the touch**, findable by a slow hover-temperature cue: it's Eleanor's copy of poems, inscribed "To E, from T — *obligation is love made visible*"); the fireplace (enterable; looking UP reveals a hoist rope tied off inside the flue with a **flue lever**); the ash (sieve it: a **burnt page fragment** — a corner of the black book that Eleanor tried to burn in 1887: a half-name and the letters "...AH"); **THE ANONYMOUS SHELF** (one shelf of blank-spined volumes — pull any: dense handwriting, no attribution, never the same page twice; an entire shelf of anonymous testimony. It **cannot be photographed** — the camera returns blur — the one collection the evidence board refuses. "Some things were for her alone.").
**Items:** **Hoist rope** (chandelier puzzle); **burnt page fragment** (evidence item); **warm book** (slot it back wrong-side-out later in Act 3 to mark the shelf that conceals the wing's second entrance — long-game seed).
**Puzzle:** None formal — this is a discovery room. The temperature-hunt for the warm book teaches the examine-everything instinct.
**Recorder yield:** Pages turning. Hundreds of them, softly, all at once.
**Role:** Source of the rope; plants the burning-failed backstory before the player ever meets Eleanor.

## 2.4 STUDY (Thomas's public study)
**Read:** A respectable merchant's office: ledgers in ranked leather, a partners desk, a wool-trade map. Everything about this room performs honesty. The desk has a locked deep drawer with a four-digit letter lock.
**Dressing:** Framed commendations; a weighing scale; a decanter, one glass used, one clean.
**Interactive:** TWO SETS OF LEDGERS — shelf A (display ledgers) and, found by pulling the wool-trade map (it's on a roller), shelf B recessed behind it (the real ledgers). Same months, different numbers; the desk blotter (mirror-writing impression readable with the Washroom mirror later — optional shortcut); the **tape measure** in the desk tray (free to take — the measuring mechanic unlocks here).
**Items:** **Tape measure** (mechanic item); **brass crank handle** (in the locked drawer); **letter to Dr. Whitmore, unsent** (evidence: "Marcus — she has seen it. I require your discretion once more — T.A.").
**Puzzle:** TWO SETS OF BOOKS — compare display ledger vs. real ledger for 1887: every month matches except one entry, **September**, where the real ledger shows a payment to "M.W." The discrepancy date **14-09** is the drawer code (entered as letters N-I-D? no — entered on the letter-lock as the ledger's column cipher, spelled **WOOL** → the lock's letters are keyed to the ledger's own column headings; tutorialised by a crossed-out practice line on the blotter). Drawer opens: crank handle + the Whitmore letter.
**Recorder yield:** A pen scratching. It stops when you stop walking. Resumes when you move.
**Role:** Gives both mechanic (tape measure) and the chandelier's second component; establishes Thomas's two-faced bookkeeping = the literal "two sets of books" theme.

## 2.5 KITCHEN (Mara's base camp)
**Read:** Stone-flagged, scarred central table, the only room that feels *currently lived in* — Mara's laptop bag, kettle, the recorder dock. A small window over the sink. In the corner: the **dumbwaiter** shaft, its car stuck somewhere between floors, a frayed pull-rope hanging.
**Dressing:** Copper pans; a meat safe; a pantry with one modern shopping bag against a century of empty shelves.
**Interactive:** The TABLE = save point + evidence board (collected documents pin up here, strings auto-draw between linked evidence — the game's "journal" rendered diegetically); the recorder dock (review all recordings); the kettle (make tea: restores nothing, it's just *nice*, and the house always cools it too fast — a one-line dread joke); **THE WALL** (press your palm flat against the plaster — tutorialised here, repeatable anywhere, forever: the wall moves. Fractional but definite. Five seconds in, five seconds out. The house breathes — a global ambient system, subtle displacement plus sound bed on a ten-second cycle, which **slows after publication**. The player's recurring "is it still breathing?" check); the dumbwaiter (see puzzle); the pantry (contains **flat irons ×3** and **lamp oil**).
**Items:** **Lamp oil** (refills the lamp — soft pressure, never a fail state); **flat irons** (dumbwaiter counterweights); **matches**.
**Puzzle:** THE DUMBWAITER — the car is stuck because its counterweight sash is empty. Load flat irons into the counterweight basket (accessible through the inspection hatch) one at a time; three irons brings the car down *past* the kitchen to the cellar level — wrong — remove one; **two irons** aligns it with the kitchen hatch. Inside the car: a tray with a dried-up supper, a servant's note ("She rings at all hours now. I shan't go up after dark again — A."), and the **back stair key** on a household ring. *Gates 2nd Floor West.* (The one-iron / three-iron states each give a different audio sting from inside the shaft — the shaft carries sound from both floors; players will ride this discovery later.)
**Recorder yield:** The 2:47 voice — first clear capture; this is the scripted Act 1 night event, mirroring Episode 2 of the series.
**Role:** Home. Save, review, plan. The dumbwaiter is Act 1's keystone puzzle.

## 2.6 WEST WING HALL (the corridor that tells the truth)
**Read:** The long windowless corridor connecting kitchen/library/dining to the heart of the house. Burgundy runner, unlit sconces, and at its far end — nothing dramatic. It's just a hall. That's the trick.
**Interactive:** This is the tape-measure tutorial space: measured, the hall is exactly what the floor plan says. A wall-mounted framed copy of the **floor plan itself** hangs here (the player's map item — taking it adds the map UI, and the map shows the compass rose: first chance to notice the Compass Lie); the cellar door is at its kitchen end (see 2.7).
**Items:** **The floor plan** (map UI unlock).
**Puzzle:** None — a control sample. Every honest measurement here makes the dishonest ones later land harder.
**Recorder yield:** Doors opening and closing in sequence down the hall, none of which move.

## 2.7 CELLAR STAIRS (landing + the door that won't)
**Read:** A plain plank door at the corridor's end. No lock plate. No keyhole. No handle — just a smooth worn patch where a handle should be. It does not open. Examine: "It isn't stuck. It's *held*."
**Interactive:** The door (push/pull/shoulder — scripted refusals, escalating examine text across acts: Act 2 — "The hinges have been freshly oiled. By whom?"; Act 3 — a thin breath of cold rose-scented air under it); the gap under the door (slide a paper under, like the old key trick — it is *taken*).
**Puzzle/Gate:** STORY GATE — the cellar cannot be opened by any item. After the Act 3 sealed-wing climax (reading Eleanor's journal at her desk), returning here finds the door **standing open**, twelve steps down into dark. This is deliberate design: the player must learn that the house grants access when witnessed correctly. The paper-under-the-door interaction, if performed in Act 1, is returned in Act 4 — the same sheet slides back out, with handwriting on it.
**Role:** The game's longest fuse.

## 2.8 BACK STAIR (central corridor, by the Master Bedroom)
**Read:** A narrow servant's stair behind a door painted shut decades ago, paint cracked along the frame like a healed scar. A humble iron lock.
**Interactive:** The door (locked + sealed: needs the **back stair key** (dumbwaiter) AND scoring the paint seam with the **silver letter opener** — a two-item gate so neither find trivially solves it); the stair beyond (creaks in a five-note pattern — the same five notes the Nursery music box plays; players who notice get chills for free).
**Role:** Act 1 exit. Leads to 2nd Floor West (servants' quarters) and onward to the attic.

## 2.9 MASTER BEDROOM
**Read:** Thomas and Eleanor's room. A marriage rendered in furniture: his side regimented, hers untouched-as-museum. A cheval mirror angled at the door. Her jewellery box on the vanity — a puzzle box with an empty oval recess in its lid.
**Dressing:** A pressed rose under glass; his razor strop; the bed made with hospital corners (the same corners as the room in the sealed wing — set dressing rhyme).
**Interactive:** The cheval mirror (periodically, the reflected door stands open when the real one is shut — pure dread, no mechanics); the loose floorboard on Eleanor's side (pry with letter opener: **journal page**, torn out — entry for *August 1887*: "I have hidden the rest where Thomas will never look: in plain sight of God."→ points to the Chapel); his wardrobe (a locked travel case — opens with the same WOOL cipher, teaching code-reuse: inside, **train tickets dated Sept 13–15, 1887** — the alibi, as evidence).
**Items:** **Journal page (Aug 1887)**; **train tickets** (evidence).
**Puzzle:** THE JEWELLERY BOX — the lid's oval recess takes the **mourning brooch** (Dining Room). Set it in and the box's mechanism plays four notes and opens: inside, a **locket missing its photograph** and a tiny key stamped **"C"** (the Chapel register's clasp key). The locket's photo is found in the Conservatory; reuniting them (combine) is an *optional* emotional sidequest with an achievement: **Witnessed**.
**Recorder yield:** Two breathing patterns. One stops abruptly at 2:47.

## 2.10 WASHROOM (between Master Bedroom and Nursery)
**Read:** Cold tile, a copper tub, a shaving mirror over the basin. The room steams up on its own at night.
**Interactive:** The mirror (when the room is steamed — any visit between 2:00–3:00 AM, or by boiling the kettle and carrying it in, a player-discoverable trick — writing appears in the condensation: a tally, **47 strokes**, struck through in groups); the mirror also reads the Study blotter's mirror-writing (optional shortcut to the WOOL cipher); the basin trap (unscrew: a **small radiator key** — winds the Nursery music box).
**Items:** **Radiator key / winding key**.
**Recorder yield:** Water draining, endlessly, from a tub that holds none.

## 2.11 NURSERY
**Read:** A nursery that was never used. The cradle still wrapped in its delivery cloth. The rocking horse faces the wall, deliberately. A music box on the shelf, its winding socket empty.
**Dressing:** Unopened alphabet blocks; a christening gown boxed with its receipt; wallpaper of songbirds — the same birds as the Entrance Hall, but here every painted cage is *open*.
**Interactive:** The rocking horse (turning it to face the room is the player's choice — doing so triggers the game's single most-discussed event: nothing happens. Until you leave and return, and it faces the wall again); the cradle (do not unwrap — the game refuses: "Some things Mara will not do."); the music box (see puzzle).
**Items:** none taken — this room only gives *music*.
**Puzzle:** THE LULLABY — wind the music box with the **winding key** (Washroom). It plays five notes and sticks. The full melody is heard ONLY on a 2:47 recording made in the Conservatory (Eleanor hums it). The music box has a manual crank-advance: the player must finish the tune by ear, advancing through note choices — an audio puzzle, forgiving, three slots. Completing the lullaby opens the music box's false bottom: **a small portrait photograph** (fits the locket) and **half a register page** (the births/deaths page torn from the Chapel register — listing a death the official record doesn't contain).
**Recorder yield:** The five sticking notes, completed by a hummed sixth, seventh, eighth — in a woman's voice.

## 2.12 CHAPEL ROOM (anteroom)
**Read:** A small family chapel vestibule: prayer kneeler, a lectern with the **Ashford family register** (births, marriages, deaths) chained and clasped, a wall of small memorial plaques, and a niche behind a wrought-iron grille. Five candle spikes stand before the plaques, cold.
**Interactive:** The register (unlock clasp with key **"C"** — one page is torn out (its half is in the Nursery); legible deaths each have a date); the plaques (names + years, including three the register doesn't list); the grille niche (no visible lock — it is candle-driven).
**Puzzle:** LIGHT FOR THE DEAD — five candle spikes, candle stubs from the Dining Room + matches from the Kitchen. Light candles in **order of death dates**, assembled from register + plaques + the torn half-page (so the puzzle requires the Nursery chain first). Correct sequence: the grille's mechanism releases — inside the niche, wrapped in altar cloth: **the iron key**, heavy, brown-tagged in faded copperplate: **"West Wing."** Taking it, one examine line honours the novel's manifesting key: *"The key ring in her pocket is heavier. She didn't put it there."* — the niche held the key; the house added it to her ring. Granted, not found. The compass on the player's map says the sealed wing is east. The tag says west. Neither is wrong. *Gates the sealed wing — Act 3.*
**Recorder yield:** Latin, badly pronounced, by a man's voice that keeps stopping to listen.

## 2.13 CONSERVATORY (the unlabelled north-east room — the murder room)
**Read:** A glass-roofed garden room: rows of planting benches, terracotta pots and tubs of century-dead **lemon trees** — and at the centre bench, **one lemon tree alive**, impossibly, fruit slowly ripening in soil nothing has watered since 1887. The cold here is structural. This is where Thomas murdered Eleanor — her blood soaked into the soil around these trees (journal, March 1887). *Canon note: the rose motif belongs exclusively to the sealed wing; the Conservatory is citrus. Murder room smells of lemons; the waiting room smells of roses.*
**Dressing:** Frost ferning on the glass in high summer; a fallen pruning stool nobody rights (it re-falls); a watering can full of water that never goes stale.
**Interactive:** The living lemon tree (its fruit cannot be picked — Mara's hands refuse; examine: "Mara's hands won't do it. Hers, or someone's."); the centre bench tub (turn it: pressed into the underside of the soil, **the locket photograph**'s glass-plate original — and the potting bench drawer holds the **missing county atlas page** from the Library, folded around a pressed lemon leaf); the glass panes (breathe on / lamp-warm a specific pane and an etched message blooms in the frost: **"HE TOLD THEM I FELL"**).
**Items:** **Locket photograph**; **atlas page** (evidence — the estate boundary on it is drawn *differently* from the official plan).
**Puzzle:** None mechanical — the Conservatory is the recorder's proving ground: any recording left here yields the strongest, clearest 2:47 material in the game, including the lullaby needed for the Nursery, and (post-Act 3) full sentences. The room is the game's emotional core and its audio key-shop.
**Recorder yield:** Tiered by act — Act 1: the lullaby. Act 2: "the conservatory, the conservatory" repeated like a correction whenever a recording elsewhere says "staircase." Act 3+: testimony.

## 2.14 LOCKED EAST WING (overview only — full bible in Volume 2)
The iron-banded doors open with the Chapel's iron key, Act 3. Beyond: the corridor that measures forty-two feet inside and twenty-eight outside, five doors (bare bedroom · sewing room · second nursery · **the locked rose-scented room, which IS Thomas's private study** (canon ruling) · Eleanor's room at the corridor's end). Full design: Volume 5. **Recorder at these doors, once the iron key exists:** the absence-silence is replaced by the loop — *"I will tell you what happened. I will tell you what happened."* — then paper rustling, then a pen, writing down that you arrived. **Recruitment lore, named:** every tenancy in the house's history was processed by the same village firm — **Caldwell and Associates** (see Vol 4: the December First visit; the black book, page 41). This wing gets its own volume once its floor map exists — it deliberately does NOT appear in detail on the ground floor plan; the plan shows it as hatched void, which is correct and canon: the surveyor couldn't draw it either.

# 4 · MAP-GENERATION PROMPTS (match your parchment style)

Use these to generate the three remaining maps. Keep your established style anchor at the front of each prompt:

**STYLE ANCHOR (paste first, every time):**
> Aged parchment architectural floor plan, hand-inked Victorian drafting style, fine sepia linework with cross-hatched walls, torn and foxed parchment edges, dark smoky vignette surrounding the plan, elegant serif title banner at top, ornate compass rose at lower right with North marked, room names in italic serif lettering, subtle ink stains and fold creases, 19th-century surveyor's document aesthetic.

### MAP PROMPT A — THE CELLAR
> [STYLE ANCHOR] Title banner: "ASHFORD MANOR — CELLAR PLAN — SURVEYED 1885". An irregular underground level, smaller than the house above, drawn as thick rough-hewn foundation walls. Rooms: at top, "Cellar Stairs" descending twelve drawn steps from the floor above; a long central "Undercroft" with drawn support pillars; west side, a "Coal Room" with a chute hatch to outside and a "Wine Cellar" with drawn racking; east side, a "Cold Store" and a small "Well Room" with a circular well drawn in plan; at the far south end, a separate chamber labelled "Foundation Chamber" containing a drawn stone shelf alcove along its wall; between Undercroft and Foundation Chamber, a narrow passage marked "Bricked Archway (blocked)". One additional passage at the east edge drawn fading into the vignette darkness, deliberately unlabelled and unfinished, its walls trailing off into nothing. Hand-inked annotation near the Foundation Chamber in different, hurried handwriting: "do not store goods here". Scale bar bottom left. The compass rose at lower right is drawn smudged, as if the ink refused to dry.

*(Design intent: Coal Room = the "behind the coal room" beat from the book; Foundation Chamber = the original book's home; the unfinished fading passage = the network seed for the series finale; the smudged compass = the lie continues underground.)*

### MAP PROMPT B — SECOND FLOOR
> [STYLE ANCHOR] Title banner: "ASHFORD MANOR — FIRST FLOOR PLAN — SURVEYED 1885" (British convention: the floor above ground is the First Floor). The plan is divided into two halves separated by a wall with a single connecting door marked "locked". EAST HALF (family side, reached by the grand staircase drawn at the Entrance Hall position): a "Gallery Landing" overlooking the hall below, "Guest Bedroom I", "Guest Bedroom II", "Eleanor's Morning Room" with a bay window, a "Bath Room", and a "Linen Closet". WEST HALF (servants' side, reached by the narrow back stair drawn at the central corridor position): a tight corridor labelled "Servants' Passage", four small identical rooms labelled "Maid's Room I–IV", a slightly larger "Housekeeper's Room", a "Box Room", and at its end a steep narrow stair drawn ascending, labelled "To Attics". The dumbwaiter shaft drawn as a small square marked "D.W." appearing in the Servants' Passage, aligned above the kitchen's position. Above the locked east wing's position: the plan simply stops — the parchment is drawn as torn away along that entire edge, the tear rendered as part of the document. Compass rose lower right.

*(Design intent: the torn-away edge over the sealed wing = the surveyor's plan literally cannot show what's there; the locked connecting door makes the two staircases feel like two genuinely separate areas, as you asked.)*

### MAP PROMPT C — THE ATTICS (THIRD FLOOR)
> [STYLE ANCHOR] Title banner: "ASHFORD MANOR — ATTIC PLAN — SURVEYED 1885". A long open roof-space plan drawn with diagonal rafter hatching: a central "Long Attic" running most of the length, a "Water Tank Room" with a drawn rectangular cistern, a "Trunk Store" crowded with drawn outlines of trunks and dust-sheeted shapes, and a small "Servant's Sick Room" with a single drawn bed. At the east end, a hatch and ladder marked "To Roof Walk". In the middle of the plan, clearly drawn and clearly impossible, a sealed rectangular space with no door at all, its four walls unbroken, labelled in the surveyor's neat hand: "void — no access", with a second annotation beside it in the hurried handwriting: "measured thrice. it is larger inside the measurements." Compass rose lower right, drawn correctly but with the cardinal letters N E S W in the wrong order.

*(Design intent: the doorless room is the attic's centrepiece mystery and a late-game payoff; the scrambled compass letters escalate the Compass Lie vertically.)*
