# MAP PROMPT D — THE SEALED WING
## Eleanor's hand-drawn journal page (Caton couldn't survey it; she lived in it)

This map deliberately breaks the series' parchment-survey style. The other four plans are Caton's professional 1885 drawings; the wing has no survey — the First Floor plan is literally torn away over it, and Caton's Field Book says *"the wing declined."* So its map is a different document entirely: **a page from Eleanor's journal**, drawn from memory by the person the wing was sealed around. In-game, this is also how the player acquires it: the map UI for the wing unlocks only when the journal is taken, and it renders as this page.

**STYLE ANCHOR (paste first):**
> A single aged journal page, cream laid paper with visible chain lines, water-damage staining and buckling along the bottom edge, drawn entirely in iron-gall ink gone warm brown-black, in a careful Victorian woman's hand — precise, taught-as-moral-discipline penmanship that loosens slightly under emotion. Hand-drawn amateur floor sketch, NOT an architect's plan: wavering ruled lines done against a book's edge, rooms as simple rectangles, annotations in small neat copperplate, some words underlined twice, one line crossed out so hard the nib tore the paper. Intimate, private, candlelit document aesthetic. No compass rose. No scale bar. No title banner.

**THE PROMPT:**
> [STYLE ANCHOR] A hand-sketched floor plan of a single long corridor drawn down the page's centre, labelled in her hand: "the west passage". Along its left side, four rectangles in sequence: the first labelled "the empty room" with a small sketched bed; the second "my sewing room" with a tiny dress-form drawn standing in its corner; the third "the nursery" — and beside this word, a smaller line in fainter ink: "for the one who was coming"; the fourth rectangle drawn with its door marked by a small sketched rose and labelled "Thomas's study", the word Thomas's written slightly smaller than every other word on the page. At the corridor's end, a fifth room larger than the rest, labelled simply "mine", with a tiny drawn window and beside the window the annotation "the garden, always afternoon". Along the corridor's length, a measurement line with arrows and the note "42 ft" — followed by three words underlined twice: "(it is not)". At the corridor's entrance, double doors drawn with their hinge-strokes on the OUTSIDE, annotated "they open outward. ask why." In the bottom margin, in the loosened hand, one final line partially water-damaged: "If anyone finds this page, the rooms are exactly where I left them. So am I." One sentence elsewhere on the page begun and crossed out so violently the ink has torn through.

**Design intent baked in:** the room labels are hers, not the game's (the player's wing map UI uses her names — "mine", not "Eleanor's Room"); "for the one who was coming" pre-loads the Boston letter; the rose on the study door teaches the scent-navigation; "(it is not)" is the corridor set piece, promised in her hand before the player's tape measure proves it; the outward hinges and the always-afternoon window are her own noticing — she was the wing's first investigator, and the map makes the player her second.

**Variant (optional, for the in-game UI's "complete" state):** the same page, but after the Witnessing scene a sixth annotation has appeared in fresh dark ink beside "mine": *"witnessed."* — generate both states if your tool holds consistency; otherwise the base page is sufficient and the game can overlay the word.
