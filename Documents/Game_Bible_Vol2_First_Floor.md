# THE WEEPING WALLS — GAME BIBLE, VOLUME 2
## Ashford Manor: First Floor · Two Halves, One Locked Door

The first floor is two separate areas pretending to be one floor. The **West Half** (servants' side) opens at the end of Act 1 via the Back Stair. The **East Half** (family side) opens at the start of Act 2 via the Grand Staircase, once the chandelier is winched clear. Between them: a single connecting door, **bolted from the east side** — so the player who arrives first via the servants' side finds it immovable, and the player who later arrives via the family side can slide the bolt and join the halves into a loop. That bolt-slide is the floor's "click" moment: the manor becomes traversable, and the player feels the map fold together.

**Floor theme:** *Upstairs, Downstairs, and the room that is neither.* The servants' side is about the people who watched; the family side is about the people who were watched; and the unnumbered guest bedroom is about something that watches still.

**The torn-away plan edge** (over the sealed wing) is canon: surveyor Caton could not complete the drawing. In-game, the map UI for this floor simply ends in a tear. Measuring toward that edge with the tape measure returns the reading: *"—"*.

# WEST HALF — THE SERVANTS' SIDE (opens Act 1 finale)

## 2.1 SERVANTS' PASSAGE
**Read:** A corridor built narrow to be invisible — plain boards, no runner, walls scuffed at tray height by a century of carried suppers. On the wall by the stair head: the **bell board** — a rank of coiled bells, each labelled with a room name. One bell's label slot is **blank**.
**Dressing:** A row of coat pegs, one bonnet remaining; candle stubs in wall niches; a tray abandoned on the floor mid-corridor, facing the stairs, as if set down at a run.
**Interactive:** THE BELL BOARD — each bell can be examined; labels match ground-floor rooms; the blank bell's wire exits upward into the ceiling (toward the attic — the Wire Trace puzzle in Volume 3 starts here). At 2:47 AM, the blank bell rings. Once. Every night; the **dumbwaiter hatch (D.W.)** — the shaft's upper door. The car can be called up/sent down. Advanced recorder play: place the recorder in the car, send it down, and you've bugged the kitchen remotely — required later for capturing a ground-floor 2:47 event while the player must be elsewhere.
**Items:** **Candle box** (refills for the Chapel and lamp); **the abandoned tray** (examine: the supper is the same meal as the one found inside the dumbwaiter car — same night, two servants, both stopped mid-task).
**Puzzle:** None here — the Passage is a junction and a fuse: blank bell (attic), dumbwaiter (kitchen), four doors (maids), and the locked connecting door east.
**Recorder yield:** Bells. All of them, softly, in the order a morning's rounds would ring them — ending on the blank bell, loudest.
**Role:** The servants' hub. Establishes the floor's question: *who was the twelfth bell for?*

## 2.2 MAID'S ROOM I — ADA'S ROOM
**Read:** Neat, occupied-feeling, a room left expecting to return. A washstand, a narrow bed, a framed photograph of a family that isn't the Ashfords.
**Interactive:** Under the mattress: **Ada's diary** — the "A." of the dumbwaiter note. Entries across 1893: *"The bells ring with no one to ring them. Mrs. H says wind. There is no wind inside a wall."* Final entry: *"I am to sleep in the sick room until the fever passes. I have no fever."*
**Items:** **Ada's diary** (evidence — first independent witness; pins to the board and links itself to the dumbwaiter note).
**Recorder yield:** A girl reciting a prayer very fast, getting faster.
**Role:** Humanises the staff; aims the player at the attic sick rooms before they can reach them.

## 2.3 MAID'S ROOM II — THE STRIPPED ROOM
**Read:** Bare to the boards. No bed, no curtain — only a wardrobe too heavy to have been worth moving.
**Interactive:** The wardrobe's back panel is loose (pry with the letter opener): behind it, the **maids' hidey-hole** — a biscuit tin holding pooled wages, a pawn ticket, and a folded **house rules card** with one rule inked out by hand. Held to the lamp, the struck line reads: *"No servant shall answer any bell after midnight."* Someone removed the rule. The tin also holds a **small mirror with a handle snapped off** — fits a slot in the Housekeeper's sewing box (see 2.6).
**Items:** **Mirror head** (component); **pawn ticket** (lore: item pawned — "1 pendulum, brass" — the player now knows the clock pendulum left the house and came back).
**Recorder yield:** Whispered arithmetic — wages being divided, over and over, never coming out even.

## 2.4 MAID'S ROOM III — THE BLACKED WINDOW
**Read:** Ordinary, except the window: painted black **from the inside**, brush strokes hurried, paint run down the sill.
**Interactive:** Scrape the paint with the letter opener (a satisfying physical interaction — the player chooses how much to clear). The window overlooks the **Conservatory's glass roof**. From this height, the frost-etched message on the conservatory pane — mirrored and illegible from inside the room below — reads cleanly: **"HE TOLD THEM I FELL."** If the player hasn't found the etching yet, this is its discovery; if they have, it's confirmation — and the realisation someone blacked this window *so as not to see it*.
**Items:** none — the view is the item.
**Puzzle:** Soft puzzle — connecting two floors visually. Scraping at night instead shows the living lemon tree faintly luminous in the dark conservatory (pure reward).
**Recorder yield:** Brush strokes. Wet, fast, panicked.

## 2.5 MAID'S ROOM IV — THE BAY ROOM
**Read:** The largest maid's room, bay-windowed — a small privilege. On the floorboards, a pale **scorch shadow** in the shape of a standing person, the boards bleached rather than burnt.
**Dressing:** A bible open to a struck-through page; a jar of dried rose petals on the sill (the conservatory's roses — someone kept them).
**Interactive:** The scorch shadow (measure it with the tape: 162 cm — Eleanor's exact height from her record; the board pins the link automatically); the bay window latch (opens — the only first-floor window that does; from it, the front façade's windows can be counted from inside: the player counts **twelve** rooms with windows on this front. The façade has thirteen. The discrepancy is now the player's problem, resolved on the Roof Walk in Volume 3).
**Items:** **Jar of rose petals** (place in Eleanor's Morning Room later for an optional scene — the petals are taken overnight, replaced by fresh ones).
**Recorder yield:** Floorboards taking weight, slowly, in the shape's exact spot.

## 2.6 HOUSEKEEPER'S ROOM — THE FLOOR'S KEYSTONE
**Read:** Authority rendered in furniture: a proper desk, a proper bed, the only rug on the servants' side. On the desk, the **Household Ledger**; beside it, a **sewing box** with a curious lid — three thread spools mounted as rotating dials; on the wall, the **bell board master legend**.
**Interactive:** THE HOUSEHOLD LEDGER (open, readable): records every room's linen, candles, coal — and explains the column-cipher convention Thomas reused for the WOOL lock (a deliberate tutorial bridge: a player stuck on the Study drawer finds the method here); THE MASTER LEGEND: lists every bell with its room — the blank bell's line reads only **"— (Mr. Ashford's instruction)"**; THE SEWING BOX (see puzzle).
**Items (inside the sewing box):** **The chatelaine** — the housekeeper's full key ring: opens the **Box Room**, the **Bath Room cupboard**, the **linen presses**, and critically **the Attic stair door** (Volume 3's gate); **the clock pendulum**, wrapped in cloth with a note: *"Stopped the hall clock myself. It kept time we do not keep. — Mrs. H."*; the **mending list**.
**Puzzle:** THE SEWING BOX — three thread-spool dials, each must show a colour. The mending list pairs three garments to three rooms ("Master's grey coat — entrance hall peg · burgundy runner fringe — west wing hall · navy jumper—" the third entry is *Mara's own jumper*, in fresh ink). The garments' colours — **grey, burgundy, navy** — set the dials. The third entry being hers is the floor's quiet horror beat: the house has opened an account in her name. (The snapped **mirror head** from Room II slots into the box lid's empty holder — optional, but doing so reflects the underside of the lid where Mrs. H scratched: *"count the bells. count the rooms. they do not agree."*)
**Recorder yield:** Keys on a ring, walking rounds. They stop outside the unnumbered room's position — directly through the wall, on the east side — and do not walk on.
**Role:** The act's treasure room: chatelaine (attic gate), pendulum (clock mechanic — see below), cipher bridge, and the player's name in the house's books.

**THE PENDULUM PAYOFF:** returning the pendulum to the Entrance Hall grandfather clock restarts it — unlocking the **time mechanic**: Mara can now wait/sleep until a chosen hour at the kitchen table, including setting a 2:40 AM alarm. Before this, 2:47 events were stumbled into; now they're scheduled. The clock keeps perfect time — except it strikes thirteen at 2:47, and only then. **And it has one more job:** once the black book names Mara's date (Vol 5 — *02/12*), the clock face hosts the **December 2nd countdown** — days remaining, rendered as a small brass calendar wheel behind the glass that was always there and never moved before. Act 4 must be entered before the wheel runs out.

## 2.7 BOX ROOM — THE KEEPERS' LUGGAGE
**Read:** A small room stacked with the left-behind luggage of a century of tenants: trunks, valises, hat boxes — **fourteen** pieces, each with a faded paper tag. One is a modern wheeled suitcase.
**Interactive:** Every tag bears a name and a year — and every name matches a keeper entry in the black book (the evidence board draws the strings as each is read; the room is a database the player walks through). Every faded tag is tied with a rental agreement copy — and **every agreement carries the same signature across a century: Caldwell and Associates** (the hands change; the firm never does; the board strings them to the recruitment exhibit). The modern case is **Thomas Whitmore's** (d. November, three weeks before Mara's arrival): inside, his reading glasses, a half-finished crossword, a folded note in a doctor's hand: *"The door opens when it is finished with you. Until then, keep busy. It likes industry. — T.W."* — and a **Three Crowns beer mat**, a tenant-list written on its back in a pub landlord's hand: *Whitmore, 3 months. Chen, 2 wks. Porter. Simms, 1 wk. Vance, 3 days.* Everyone in Millworth keeps count. Now the player holds the count.
**Items:** **Whitmore's note** (evidence — and the cellar door's rules, stated plainly by the only person who lived them recently).
**Puzzle:** None — a payoff room. Optional completionist task: matching all fourteen tags to book entries earns the achievement **Lost Property**.
**Recorder yield:** Fourteen different breathing patterns, one after another, oldest to newest. Then a fifteenth: Mara's own, played back from a recording she hasn't made yet.

## 2.8 THE CONNECTING DOOR
**Read (from west):** A solid door, no give, no keyhole on this side. **(From east):** the same door with a simple slide bolt.
**Interactive:** Bolt slides only from the east (Act 2). Once open, the floor loops, and the door stays open — except some mornings it is found bolted again. The game never explains which side did it.

# EAST HALF — THE FAMILY SIDE (opens Act 2)

## 2.9 GALLERY LANDING
**Read:** A balustraded landing overlooking the Entrance Hall's two-storey drop. The raised chandelier now hangs level with the rail, close enough to touch — close enough to see clearly.
**Interactive:** THE CHANDELIER, EXAMINED PROPERLY: the chain links are **opened**, not snapped — pulled apart like taffy, the brass smeared; in the chandelier's dust, a **handprint**, palm and five fingers, too long; the winch beam above (the player's own repair, inspectable — a quiet pride beat the handprint then ruins); the balustrade (looking down re-frames the player's whole Act 1 hub from above — cheap, powerful).
**Items:** **Photograph opportunity** — Mara's phone camera (introduced here as an evidence verb): photographing the opened links and handprint creates two evidence cards. From here on, anything anomalous can be photographed; photos feed the **publish ending's completeness meter**.
**Recorder yield:** The Entrance Hall footsteps heard in Act 1 — but from up here, they're overhead. They are always one floor above the player.
**Role:** Re-frames Act 1; introduces the camera; quietly establishes that "above" doesn't run out where the house does.

## 2.10 GUEST BEDROOM (UNNUMBERED) — THE ROOM WITH NO NUMBER
**Read:** Between the stairwell and Guest Bedroom I: a guest room better kept than any room in the house. Bed turned down. Water in the ewer — fresh. No number on the door. No entry in the Household Ledger. No bell label. The room the house keeps ready.
**Dressing:** A guest book on the side table; a posy of conservatory roses, alive, in a glass; towels warm to the touch.
**Interactive:** THE GUEST BOOK — dozens of signatures spanning two centuries, different names, different inks… and (a handwriting-comparison interaction with collected documents) **every signature is in the same hand**. The latest line is blank, the pen beside it full; Mara cannot be made to sign — the interaction exists, hovers, and her hand refuses (mirror of the Conservatory secateurs); THE OVERNIGHT EXCHANGE (see puzzle); the window (this room's window is one of the twelve — but measured, the room is **3 feet shorter** than its exterior wall; the first measurable lie on this floor, foreshadowing the wing).
**Puzzle:** THE GUEST'S TRADE — any single item left on the bed overnight is gone by morning, replaced. The trades are authored, not random: leave the **warm Library book** → returned with a pressed-rose bookmark marking a now-legible inscription page; leave the **jar of rose petals** → returned full of *fresh* petals; leave the **burnt page fragment** → returned **less burnt** — a few more letters readable ("…ASHFORD, E…"). One trade is required for the critical path (the fragment); the rest are the game's strangest optional economy. Leave the recorder overnight and it returns blank — eight hours erased — with the battery full.
**Recorder yield (in the room, attended):** Nothing. Total absence — the same dead silence as the sealed wing's door. The quietest room in the game is the loudest clue.
**Role:** The floor's centrepiece mystery. Never explained. The dev rule for this room: **it answers questions only with better questions.**

## 2.11 GUEST BEDROOM I + BATH ROOM — WHITMORE'S ROOM
**Read:** Recently inhabited: a made bed gone slightly stale, a dressing gown on the door, a doctor's travelling bag on the stand. The adjoining Bath Room is Victorian splendour gone cold — a vast tub, a marble basin, a mirrored cupboard (chatelaine-locked).
**Interactive:** WHITMORE'S BAG (simple latch): his **dosage diary** — a physician charting his own decline with clinical calm, entries cross-referenced to dates the player can match against the black book's final pre-Mara entries: *he knew his date and kept appointments anyway*; his **letters from a descendant's conscience**: drafts to a medical journal confessing what his ancestor Dr. Marcus Whitmore signed in 1887 — the false coroner's report — never sent. THE MIRRORED CUPBOARD (chatelaine): laudanum bottles a century old AND one modern prescription bottle, label printed with a dispensing date — **the day after Whitmore died**.
**Items:** **Whitmore's confession drafts** (evidence — the publish ending's keystone document: a Whitmore indicting a Whitmore, closing the 1887 circle); **dosage diary** (evidence).
**Recorder yield (Bath Room):** A tap running; under it, a man's voice rehearsing the same sentence with small revisions — the confession being drafted aloud, draft after draft, forever.
**Role:** Converts Whitmore from a name in a book to the game's tragic conscience, and hands the player the document the entire publish ending pivots on.

## 2.12 GUEST BEDROOM II — THE 1924 ROOM
**Read:** A child's room frozen mid-departure: a trunk half packed, toys arranged for a game with rules only the player can guess at, pencil height-marks climbing the door frame.
**Interactive:** THE HEIGHT MARKS: dated 1921–1924, ordinary — then three more above them, undated, continuing past any child's height, the last at 6'4"; THE TOY THEATRE: a cardboard proscenium with cut-out figures mid-scene — a house, a lady, a man — with one figure cleanly **missing from its slot** (its match is in an attic trunk; restoring it is optional and triggers the toy theatre's one scene: the paper lady descends the paper stairs — and the paper house's thirteenth window opens); the trunk (child's things, a 1924 newspaper wrapping — its visible headline a small dig of period flavour, and the family's surname matching a Box Room luggage tag: they left their keeper behind).
**Items:** **Newspaper page** (evidence — dates the 1924 departure); height-mark photo opportunity.
**Recorder yield:** A counting game — "…coming, ready or not" — and then a very long silence in which someone is definitely still hiding.
**Role:** The 1924 family beat from the lore made walkable; pure dread room with one optional toy payoff.

## 2.13 ELEANOR'S MORNING ROOM
**Read:** Her daylight room, the warm twin to the sealed wing's cold one: bay window full of pale light, a writing desk (the exact twin of the desk the player will find in Act 3), an embroidery hoop mid-stitch, correspondence sorted into pigeonholes.
**Dressing:** The embroidery, examined, is mid-word: needle parked in the upright of a letter — the stitched line so far reads *"SOMEONE MUST KNO"*; a small portrait of Thomas, face turned to the wall (the player can turn it back; by next visit it has turned again — rhyming with the nursery rocking horse).
**Interactive:** THE PIGEONHOLE DRAFTS (puzzle); the desk (its drawer is empty except a sheet of blotting paper — readable with the Washroom mirror OR the sewing-box mirror: it blots the journal's final line, *"Thomas is in his study. I am going to—"*, proving where the journal's last entry was actually written: *here*, in daylight, in her own room — she walked to the wing knowingly); the window seat (lifts: **Eleanor's seal**, her personal wax signet).
**Items:** **Eleanor's seal** (evidence-authentication item: pressed against any document on the evidence board, it verifies or refutes the handwriting — the in-fiction mechanic by which the player "proves" documents for the publish meter); **the ordered drafts** (evidence bundle).
**Puzzle:** THE ESCALATION — her unsent letter drafts to the magistrate are scattered across pigeonholes, undated. Each draft's paper carries a **watermark** visible against the bay window light; the Household Ledger's stationery purchases date each watermark. Ordering the drafts chronologically plays her voice reading each — the language hardening from petition to accusation to, in the last draft, certainty: *"…and if I am not believed in life, I will be believed in whatever follows it."* Completing the order pins the full bundle to the evidence board as a single keystone exhibit.
**Recorder yield:** Pen scratching — and here's the floor's best audio trick: it is in **call-and-response with the Study's pen** one floor below. Record both rooms in the same night (dumbwaiter bug + this room) and the two recordings interleave into one rhythm: two people writing against each other, 1887, his ledger versus her letters.
**Role:** Eleanor as author, not victim — the room that makes the Act 3 wing land as a homecoming instead of a haunting. Gives the publish ending its verification mechanic.

# 3 · FLOOR PUZZLE ECONOMY

WEST (Act 1 finale): Back Stair → Passage (bell board, D.W.) → Ada's diary (I) → mirror head + struck rule (II) → blacked window reveal (III) → scorch + window count problem (IV) → **SEWING BOX → CHATELAINE + PENDULUM** → Box Room (Whitmore's note) → blocked at the connecting door + the locked Attic stair (chatelaine now in hand — the attic opens when the player chooses, Volume 3).

EAST (Act 2): Grand Staircase → Gallery (camera verb, handprint) → unnumbered room (the trade: burnt fragment upgrade — critical path) → Whitmore's room (**confession drafts** — publish keystone) → 1924 room → Morning Room (**seal + ordered drafts** — verification mechanic + keystone exhibit) → **slide the bolt** → the floor loops.

Feeds onward: chatelaine → Attics (Vol 3) · pendulum → clock → scheduled 2:47s (whole game) · confession + seal + drafts → publish meter (Vol 4's endings) · blank bell + wire → the attic Wire Trace · the twelve-vs-thirteen window count → the Roof Walk.

# 4 · BUILD NOTES
The two halves should feel materially different: west = bare boards, whitewash, candle-scale lighting; east = runner carpets, papered walls, lamp-scale lighting. Same footsteps sound different per half. The unnumbered room belongs to neither palette — it is *hotel-perfect*, and that wrongness is the read. The connecting door's two faces (blank vs. bolted) should be modelled as genuinely different meshes; players will screenshot it.
